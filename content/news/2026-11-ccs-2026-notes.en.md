I am at ACM CCS 2026 in The Hague this week. I have mostly been attending sessions on agentic AI security. With agents still loosely defined, the proposals range from hardening the model to prompt-level defenses to harnesses that constrain execution from outside. Across the talks, though, one direction stood out: "The model cannot protect itself. Execution lives outside the model."

- A keynote presented information-flow control (IFC), which labels data and lets a policy engine allow or deny each tool call deterministically
- Several talks argued that safety belongs at the model level and security at the system level, though that split has plenty of critics
- A knowledge base for secure coding, or even a single line in an MCP tool description, was raised as a possible attack surface
- One design sends only the high-risk actions of a VLA robot, with context, to a separate authorization server, close to the separation of intelligence and control in our [AEGIS-RM](/en/research/aegis-rm/) project

For factory robots and industrial agents, where should execution authority sit, and at which stage should security live: the model, the agent, or before and after the model? The full post covers the talks, references and a few observations about the conference itself.
