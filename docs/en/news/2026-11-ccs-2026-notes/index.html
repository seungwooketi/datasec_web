Notes from ACM CCS 2026 - agentic AI security moves outside the model

Read the full article ↗The original, with figures, tables and references

This article is excerpted from a post on Center Director Seungwoo Kum's personal blog. The original is available at seungwoo.datasec.work/blog/2026/ccs-2026-notes.

I am at ACM CCS 2026 in The Hague this week. In the ML and Security sessions I attended, agentic AI security dominated. With agents still loosely defined, the proposals range from hardening the model to prompt-level defenses to harnesses that constrain execution from outside. Across the talks, though, one direction stood out: "The model cannot protect itself. Execution lives outside the model."

  • A keynote presented information-flow control (IFC), which labels data and lets a policy engine allow or deny each tool call deterministically
  • Several talks argued that safety belongs at the model level and security at the system level
  • Safety alignment proved thin enough to be pruned away neuron by neuron, and both a knowledge base for secure coding and a single line in an MCP tool description turned out to be attack surfaces
  • One design sends only the high-risk actions of a VLA robot, with context, to a separate authorization server, close to the separation of intelligence and control in our AEGIS-RM project

For industrial agents and robots, where outside the model, and at what granularity, should execution authority sit? The full post covers the talks, references and a few observations about the conference itself.
